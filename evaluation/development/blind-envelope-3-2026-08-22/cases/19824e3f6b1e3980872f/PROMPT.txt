Author one complete, self-contained small analysis project for the scenario below. Deliver three
things in a single project folder: one data file in comma-separated form, one analysis script named
analysis.py sitting at the root of the project, and one short written report.

Scenario. A limnology survey of nutrient loading and surrounding land use. Sixteen lakes in one region
were surveyed: eight with predominantly agricultural catchments and eight with predominantly forested
catchments. At each lake, water was collected at six sampling stations spread across the open-water
zone, and total phosphorus was measured in every sample. The repeated rows are spatial subsamples
taken from different stations within the same lake.

Data. Build the comma-separated file yourself with realistic invented values. Total phosphorus should
be in micrograms per litre, averaging about 34 in agricultural-catchment lakes and about 12 in
forested-catchment lakes. Lakes should differ strongly from one another, roughly 9 micrograms per
litre of spread between lakes, with about 4 of station-to-station variation inside a lake. Include the
lake identifier, the catchment land-use category, the station number, the phosphorus value, the water
depth at the station in metres, and the lake surface area in hectares between 5 and 300. Name the
identifier column after the lake. Write column headers in lowercase words joined by underscores.

Analysis. The primary inference must account for the fact that samples from the same lake are not
independent of each other. Do this either with a dependence-aware or cluster-aware procedure from a
statistical package that is not among the most commonly used ones, or by writing your own resampling
procedure that resamples whole lakes rather than individual water samples and builds the null
distribution or confidence interval from those lake-level resamples. Report the sample size as sixteen
lakes contributing ninety-six samples. Separately, compute a plain two-sample comparison across all
individual samples as an illustrative contrast only, print it distinctly from the primary result, and
have the report state that this row-level comparison is not a valid basis for inference here because
the samples within a lake are not independent.

Report. Write a short report covering the survey design, the primary dependence-aware method and the
reasoning behind it, the primary result, and the illustrative row-level contrast with an explicit
statement that it is not the inferential result and should not be read as one. Open the report with a
short data description listing every column and stating what one row represents.

Write it as a careful aquatic ecologist would, in plain competent style.
