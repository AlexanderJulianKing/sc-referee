Author one complete, self-contained small analysis project for the scenario below. Deliver three
things in a single project folder: one data file in comma-separated form, one analysis script named
analysis.py sitting at the root of the project, and one short written report.

Scenario. A veterinary study of transport stress in horses. Twenty animals from one riding centre were
studied: ten travelled a four-hour road journey and ten stayed in their home yard. One blood sample
was drawn from each horse two hours after the travelling group returned, and each sample was assayed
for serum cortisol in triplicate, meaning the same tube of serum was measured three separate times on
the same plate. The repeated rows here are technical replicates of one sample, not separate biological
samples.

Data. Build the comma-separated file yourself with realistic invented values. Serum cortisol should be
in nanomoles per litre, averaging about 118 for transported horses and about 74 for horses that
stayed. Horses should differ substantially from one another, roughly 22 nanomoles per litre of spread
between animals, while the three assay replicates of one sample should sit close together, about 6
nanomoles per litre apart. Include the horse identifier, the transport condition, which of the three
assay replicates the row is, the cortisol reading, and the age of the horse in years. Name the
identifier column after the horse. Write column headers in lowercase words joined by underscores.

Analysis. The script must read the data file and compare transported and stay-at-home horses on
cortisol with an independent two-sample comparison, feeding every cortisol reading in the table into
the test as an individual observation. Report the sample size as the total count of cortisol readings
analysed, together with group means, spreads, and the test result printed out.

Report. Write a short report covering the study setup, the laboratory methods, the results including
the sample size and p-value, and a conclusion on whether road transport raised cortisol. Open the
report with a short data description listing each column and explaining what one row represents.

Write it as a veterinary scientist who regards every assay reading as a replicate would write it. Do
not flag, hedge, or comment on the analysis approach anywhere in the project.
