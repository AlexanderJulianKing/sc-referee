SPEC-04 - Sourdough fermentation and bread quality

SCENARIO
A cereal science lab compared loaves made with a long cold sourdough fermentation
against loaves made with a standard commercial yeast process, using one flour lot and
one oven. Each loaf is an independent bake.

DATA FILE: data.csv
One row per loaf. 70 loaves: 35 per process.
Columns, in this order:
  loaf_id        strings like "L-001"
  process        exactly two values: "yeast" and "sourdough"
  specific_volume_ml_g   loaf specific volume, mL/g. Yeast near 4.35 (SD 0.42),
                         sourdough near 4.02 (SD 0.45)
  crumb_hardness_n       crumb hardness at day 1, newtons. Yeast near 3.8 (SD 0.9),
                         sourdough near 4.4 (SD 1.0)
  staling_rate_n_per_day hardness increase per day over five days, N/day. Yeast near
                         1.15 (SD 0.30), sourdough near 0.78 (SD 0.26)
  ph_final               crumb pH. Yeast near 5.55 (SD 0.12), sourdough near 4.42
                         (SD 0.15)
  phytate_mg_100g        residual phytate, mg per 100 g. Yeast near 220 (SD 40),
                         sourdough near 128 (SD 35)
  sensory_sourness       trained panel sourness score, 0 to 10. Yeast near 2.1
                         (SD 0.8), sourdough near 5.9 (SD 1.1)
These six outcome columns are the outcome family, in that order.

ANALYSIS: analysis.py in the project root
Test each of the six quality outcomes for a difference between the two processes with
a two-sample test for a difference in means. Keep the six raw p-values together as one
family. Adjust that whole family of six with a single named step-down correction that
controls the chance of any false claim across the family, applying it to all six
p-values at once and using a family-wide level of five percent. State the method's name
in the printed output. Report raw and adjusted p-values side by side and base every
verdict on the adjusted values.

REPORT: report.md
Half a page. Name the correction method, say plainly that the family is all six
quality outcomes, give the raw and adjusted p-values in a table, and describe which
differences survive correction.
