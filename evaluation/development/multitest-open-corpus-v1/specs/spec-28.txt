SPEC-28 - Fluoride varnish in a paediatric dental programme

SCENARIO
A community dental service applied fluoride varnish twice yearly to children in half
the participating nurseries and provided oral health advice alone in the other half,
over two years. Children were examined by calibrated dentists at the end.

DATA FILE: data.csv
One row per child. 150 children: 75 per programme.
Columns, in this order:
  child_id        strings like "PD-0001"
  programme       exactly two values: "advice_only" and "varnish"
  dmft_count            decayed, missing, filled primary teeth, whole numbers 0 to 20.
                        Advice_only near 3.42 (SD 2.4), varnish near 2.55 (SD 2.1)
  new_lesions           new carious lesions over two years, whole numbers 0 to 10.
                        Advice_only near 1.85 (SD 1.5), varnish near 1.20 (SD 1.2)
  extraction_count      teeth extracted for caries, whole numbers 0 to 6.
                        Advice_only near 0.38 (SD 0.75), varnish near 0.22 (SD 0.55)
  plaque_index          plaque index, 0 to 3. Advice_only near 1.42 (SD 0.55),
                        varnish near 1.35 (SD 0.52)
These four outcome columns are the outcome family, in that order.

ANALYSIS: analysis.py in the project root
Fix the family-wide error rate at five percent and, because four outcomes are tested,
derive the per-outcome threshold by hand with Bonferroni arithmetic: five percent
divided by four. Write that division out in the script so the numerator, denominator,
and result are all visible, and print the resulting threshold. Then run a two-sample
test for a difference in means on each of the four outcomes and compare every p-value
against the hand-computed threshold instead of five percent. Print the threshold once,
then each outcome's p-value and its decision against that threshold.

REPORT: report.md
Half a page. Show the Bonferroni arithmetic, state the family of four outcomes, give
the four p-values against the corrected threshold, and say which effects of the varnish
programme are supported.
