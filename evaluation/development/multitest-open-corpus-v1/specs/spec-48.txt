SPEC-48 - Two milling methods and wholegrain flour quality

SCENARIO
A grain science laboratory compared stone-milled and roller-milled wholegrain wheat
flour from the same grain lot, milled on alternating days over three weeks. Each flour
sub-sample drawn from the stream is one observation.

DATA FILE: data.csv
One row per sub-sample. 66 sub-samples: 33 per milling method.
Columns, in this order:
  sample_id       strings like "FL-001"
  milling         exactly two values: "roller" and "stone"
  damaged_starch_pct    damaged starch, percent. Roller near 6.8 (SD 0.9), stone near
                        9.4 (SD 1.2)
  water_absorption_pct  farinograph water absorption, percent. Roller near 62.5
                        (SD 2.0), stone near 66.1 (SD 2.3)
  particle_size_d50_um  median particle size, micrometres. Roller near 148 (SD 22),
                        stone near 196 (SD 30)
  tocopherol_mg_kg      total tocopherols, mg/kg. Roller near 38.5 (SD 5.5), stone
                        near 44.2 (SD 6.0)
  falling_number_s      falling number, seconds. Roller near 342 (SD 38), stone near
                        318 (SD 40)
These five outcome columns are the outcome family, in that order.

ANALYSIS: analysis.py in the project root
Set the family-wide error rate at five percent and derive the per-outcome threshold by
hand with the Sidak formula for five tests: one minus the fifth root of ninety-five
percent. Write the exponent and the subtraction out in the script so the arithmetic is
visible, and print the resulting threshold to six decimal places along with the number
of tests it assumes. Run a two-sample test for a difference in means on each of the
five outcomes and compare every p-value with that hand-computed threshold rather than
with five percent, printing each outcome's p-value and decision beneath the threshold.

REPORT: report.md
Half a page. Show the Sidak arithmetic and the threshold, state that the family is the
five flour properties, give each p-value against the threshold, and interpret the
milling comparison for bakers.
