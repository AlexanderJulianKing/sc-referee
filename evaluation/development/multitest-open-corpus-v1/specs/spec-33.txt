SPEC-33 - Standing desks in an office population

SCENARIO
An occupational health service gave sit-stand desks to staff on two floors of an office
and left two comparable floors with fixed desks, then measured activity and
musculoskeletal outcomes after three months.

DATA FILE: data.csv
One row per employee. 104 employees: 52 per desk type.
Columns, in this order:
  employee_id     strings like "EM-0001"
  desk_type       exactly two values: "fixed" and "sit_stand"
  sitting_hours_per_day  workplace sitting hours per day. Fixed near 6.4 (SD 1.1),
                        sit_stand near 4.6 (SD 1.3)
  standing_hours_per_day  workplace standing hours per day. Fixed near 0.6 (SD 0.4),
                        sit_stand near 2.3 (SD 1.0)
  low_back_pain_score   low back pain, 0 to 10. Fixed near 3.6 (SD 2.0), sit_stand
                        near 2.9 (SD 1.9)
  neck_pain_score       neck and shoulder pain, 0 to 10. Fixed near 3.2 (SD 1.9),
                        sit_stand near 3.0 (SD 1.8)
  fatigue_score         end-of-day fatigue, 0 to 10. Fixed near 4.8 (SD 1.7),
                        sit_stand near 4.4 (SD 1.8)
  productivity_self     self-rated productivity, 0 to 10. Fixed near 6.8 (SD 1.4),
                        sit_stand near 7.1 (SD 1.5)
These six outcome columns are the outcome family, in that order.

ANALYSIS: analysis.py in the project root
Put the six outcome names in one ordered list at the top of the file, then loop over
that list. Each pass of the loop selects the two desk groups for that outcome, runs a
two-sample test for a difference in means, and prints the outcome name, the two means,
the p-value, and whether it is below the conventional five-percent cutoff. The loop is
the entire inference section: nothing after it revisits the collection of six p-values,
and no correction for the number of outcomes appears anywhere.

REPORT: report.md
Half a page. Give the six outcomes with p-values and verdicts, and recommend whether
sit-stand desks should be offered to the whole building.
