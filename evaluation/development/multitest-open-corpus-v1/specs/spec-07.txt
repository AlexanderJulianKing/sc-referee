SPEC-07 - Probiotic feed additive in weaned piglets

SCENARIO
A commercial swine unit gave half of one weaning batch a Bacillus-based probiotic in
the creep feed for 28 days and left the other half on the same feed without the
additive. Pens were individually fed, and each piglet is one observation.

DATA FILE: data.csv
One row per piglet. 96 piglets: 48 per group.
Columns, in this order:
  piglet_id       strings like "PG-0001"
  diet            exactly two values: "basal" and "probiotic"
  adg_g_d               average daily gain, g/day. Basal near 342 (SD 58),
                        probiotic near 366 (SD 60)
  feed_conversion       feed-to-gain ratio, unitless. Basal near 1.68 (SD 0.18),
                        probiotic near 1.59 (SD 0.17)
  diarrhoea_days        days with scours in 28 days, whole numbers 0 to 14. Basal
                        near 3.2 (SD 2.4), probiotic near 2.1 (SD 1.9)
  faecal_lactobacilli_log  faecal lactobacilli, log10 CFU/g. Basal near 7.4 (SD 0.6),
                        probiotic near 8.0 (SD 0.6)
  serum_iga_mg_l        serum IgA, mg/L. Basal near 128 (SD 34), probiotic near 145
                        (SD 36)
These five outcome columns are the outcome family, in that order.

ANALYSIS: analysis.py in the project root
Name the five outcomes in one ordered list. In a single compact one-line collection
expression, build the list of p-values by running the two-sample difference-in-means
test for every outcome in that list. Build a second compact one-line expression that
turns those p-values into verdict strings by checking each against the conventional
five-percent cutoff. Print the outcome names paired with their p-values and verdicts.
Keep the script short; the two collection expressions are the whole testing section,
and no family-wide adjustment appears in it.

REPORT: report.md
Half a page on the trial, the five outcomes, their p-values, and which ones were
significant, with a closing note on whether the additive is worth its cost.
