SPEC-15 - Night-shift work and metabolic markers

SCENARIO
An occupational medicine survey compared permanent night-shift workers at a
distribution centre against day-shift workers at the same site, matched on age band and
job grade. Fasting bloods and anthropometry were collected at a single occupational
health visit.

DATA FILE: data.csv
One row per worker. 110 workers: 55 night, 55 day.
Columns, in this order:
  worker_id       strings like "W-1001"
  shift           exactly two values: "day" and "night"
  fasting_glucose_mmol_l  fasting glucose, mmol/L. Day near 5.12 (SD 0.55), night
                        near 5.38 (SD 0.62)
  hdl_mmol_l            HDL cholesterol, mmol/L. Day near 1.38 (SD 0.30), night near
                        1.26 (SD 0.29)
  triglycerides_mmol_l  triglycerides, mmol/L. Day near 1.32 (SD 0.60), night near
                        1.58 (SD 0.72)
  waist_cm              waist circumference, cm. Day near 92.4 (SD 11.0), night near
                        96.1 (SD 11.5)
  systolic_bp_mmhg      systolic blood pressure, mmHg. Day near 124 (SD 13), night
                        near 128 (SD 14)
  sleep_hours           self-reported sleep per 24 h, hours. Day near 7.1 (SD 1.0),
                        night near 6.2 (SD 1.2)
These six outcome columns are the outcome family, in that order.

ANALYSIS: analysis.py in the project root
Run the analysis in two clearly separated passes. In the first pass, loop over the six
outcomes, run a two-sample test for a difference in means on each, and gather the
outcome name, both group means, and the p-value into a growing container of result
records, printing nothing. In the second pass, walk that container and print the
results table, deciding at print time whether each row is significant by checking its
stored p-value against the conventional five-percent cutoff. The number of records in
the container is never used to change the cutoff.

REPORT: report.md
Half a page. Give the six-row results table with verdicts and discuss what the
significant markers imply for shift-worker health surveillance.
