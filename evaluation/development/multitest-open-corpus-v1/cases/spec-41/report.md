# Silver-impregnated versus foam dressing in neuropathic diabetic foot ulcers

## Setting

112 patients attending the vascular clinic with a neuropathic diabetic foot ulcer were
allocated at their first visit, 56 to the standard foam dressing and 56 to the
silver-impregnated dressing, and followed for twelve weeks. Five outcomes were recorded
for every patient: time to complete healing (censored at 84 days for ulcers still open at
the end of follow-up), ulcer area reduction at twelve weeks, infection episodes, pain at
dressing change, and the number of dressing changes.

## Analysis

Each outcome was compared between the two dressings with a two-sample t-test for a
difference in means. The significance level for the study was 0.05, and each p-value was
read against that level.

## Results

| Outcome | Foam | Silver | p | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Time to healing (days) | 57.96 | 49.05 | 0.0140 | significant |
| Area reduction at 12 weeks (%) | 63.28 | 72.99 | 0.0284 | significant |
| Infection episodes | 0.73 | 0.46 | 0.0594 | not significant |
| Pain at dressing change (0-10) | 3.63 | 3.58 | 0.8947 | not significant |
| Dressing changes in 12 weeks | 26.05 | 20.68 | <0.0001 | significant |

Ulcers dressed with silver closed about nine days sooner and had a larger area reduction
at twelve weeks. The clearest separation is in dressing changes: silver needed about five
fewer changes per patient over the twelve weeks, which is also the outcome with the
smallest p-value. Infection episodes were lower with silver but the difference did not
reach the study's significance level. Pain at dressing change was essentially identical
between the two arms.

## Conclusion

Three of the five outcomes favour the silver dressing at the 0.05 level, and the two that
did not reach it (infection episodes, pain) both point in a direction that is at worst
neutral for silver. The healing and dressing-change results together are the practical
case for adoption, since fewer changes means less nursing time per patient as well as less
disturbance of the wound bed. On this evidence the silver-impregnated dressing is worth
adopting for neuropathic diabetic foot ulcers in this clinic, with the caveat that a
twelve-week single-clinic comparison of this size cannot say much about infection rates,
where the observed difference is suggestive but not established.
