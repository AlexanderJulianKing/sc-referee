# Heritage and commercial starter cultures in artisan yoghurt

Eighty-four pots were sampled from the line over one week of production, 42 made with the
plant's standard commercial starter and 42 with the heritage starter, on the same milk supply
and the same vat schedule. Six quality outcomes were recorded per pot: pH at day 1, titratable
acidity, syneresis, apparent viscosity, viable lactic acid bacteria count, and consumer liking.

## Panel-level screen

The six outcomes were treated as a single panel and screened before any individual outcome was
looked at. Each outcome column was standardised over all 84 pots, the two culture mean vectors
were taken across the six standardised outcomes, and the difference between those vectors was
reduced to one number by summing its squared elements. That number was 2.671. Its null
distribution was built by permuting the culture labels 2000 times (seed 20260415) and
recomputing the same quantity. None of the 2000 permutations reached the observed value, so the
panel-level p-value is below 0.0005.

The panel therefore shows an overall culture effect, and that is what unlocked the per-outcome
comparisons below. Had the screen not cleared five percent, the analysis would have stopped
there and no individual outcome would have been reported.

## Individual outcomes

| Outcome | Commercial | Heritage | Difference | p |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| pH at day 1 | 4.420 | 4.480 | +0.060 | 0.0001 |
| Titratable acidity (%) | 0.920 | 0.860 | -0.060 | 0.0010 |
| Syneresis (%) | 6.80 | 8.40 | +1.60 | 0.0006 |
| Viscosity (mPa.s) | 1850 | 1690 | -160 | 0.0051 |
| Viable count (log10 CFU/g) | 8.35 | 8.12 | -0.23 | 0.0010 |
| Panel liking (1 to 9) | 6.40 | 6.67 | +0.27 | 0.31 |

Differences are heritage minus commercial, from Welch two-sample tests.

## What this means for culture selection

The heritage starter is the slower acidifier: higher pH at day 1 and lower titratable acidity,
consistent with its lower viable count. Its texture outcomes are the ones that matter for the
pack. Whey separation is 1.6 percentage points higher and viscosity is 160 mPa.s lower, both of
which a customer sees directly in the pot. Consumer liking did not separate the two cultures,
so the heritage starter buys no measured sensory advantage to set against the texture penalty.

On these data the commercial starter remains the better routine choice. If the heritage culture
is wanted for provenance reasons, the sensible next step is a fermentation trial that lengthens
incubation or raises the inoculation rate to close the acidification gap, then a repeat of the
syneresis and viscosity measurements. Liking should be re-measured on a larger panel before it
is used to argue either way, since this study had little power to detect a difference of a
quarter of a point on a nine-point scale.
