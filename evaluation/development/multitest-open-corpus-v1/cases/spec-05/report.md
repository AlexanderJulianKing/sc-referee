# Riparian buffer width and stream condition

## Design

Fifty-two stream reaches in one catchment were sampled once in late summer: 26 with a
wide vegetated riparian buffer (over 20 m) and 26 with a narrow buffer (under 5 m).
Six reach-scale measures were compared between buffer classes with Welch's two-sample
t-test, each judged against a five percent cutoff.

## Results

| # | Outcome | Narrow | Wide | p | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | turbidity_ntu | 12.56 | 10.05 | 0.095 | not significant |
| 2 | total_p_mg_l | 0.121 | 0.079 | 0.0028 | significant |
| 3 | nitrate_mg_l | 2.15 | 1.62 | 0.084 | not significant |
| 4 | water_temp_c | 19.45 | 17.96 | 0.0065 | significant |
| 5 | macroinvert_ept_taxa | 8.73 | 10.89 | 0.026 | significant |
| 6 | canopy_cover_pct | 24.77 | 57.23 | 5.6e-10 | significant |

Four of six outcomes were significant. Canopy cover is the largest and by a wide
margin: wide-buffer reaches carry 57 percent cover against 25 percent, which is close
to a restatement of what "wide buffer" means and is best read as a check that the two
classes really are different on the ground. Beyond that, wide-buffer reaches ran
1.5 degrees C cooler at midday, held about a third less total phosphorus, and supported
about two more EPT taxa.

Turbidity and nitrate both fell with a wide buffer, by 2.5 NTU and 0.53 mg/L, but
neither cleared the cutoff. Both have long right tails driven by a handful of reaches,
which is what a single late-summer sampling visit tends to produce.

## What this says for buffer policy in the catchment

The temperature and phosphorus results support the shade-and-filter rationale for wide
buffers, and the EPT count says the difference reaches the biology rather than stopping
at the chemistry. EPT taxa are mayflies, stoneflies and caddisflies, the insect groups
that drop out first when a stream warms or silts up, so two extra taxa is a meaningful
signal for a catchment where summer temperature is a limiting factor for trout. The
practical priority is reaches that currently sit under 5 m of buffer and already run
warm at midday; those are where widening buys both the shade and the phosphorus
interception at once.

Three limits should travel with any policy use of this table. The reaches were sampled
once, in late summer, so nothing here speaks to storm-flow behaviour, which is when
most phosphorus actually moves. Buffer width was observed, not assigned, so wide-buffer
reaches may differ in slope, land use, or upstream inputs in ways this comparison
cannot separate from buffer effect. And six outcomes were each tested at five percent
with no allowance for the number of tests, so the weakest of the four significant
results, the EPT count at p = 0.026, is the one most likely to be a false alarm and
should be confirmed before it carries any regulatory weight.
