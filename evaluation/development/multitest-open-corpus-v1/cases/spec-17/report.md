# Cold-water immersion after competitive football matches

Sixty-six semi-professional footballers each contributed one match. After the match they
either sat in a cold-water immersion bath or rested passively for the same duration,
33 players per method. Five recovery markers were measured 24 hours later. Groups were
compared with a Welch two-sample t-test on each marker; the p-value for each outcome was
compared with 0.05.

## Results at 24 hours

| Outcome | Passive | Cold water | Difference | p | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Creatine kinase (U/L) | 418.8 | 352.0 | -66.8 | 5.250e-02 | not significant |
| Soreness (0-100 VAS) | 46.0 | 36.0 | -10.0 | 1.099e-02 | significant |
| CMJ height (cm) | 34.21 | 36.00 | +1.80 | 1.216e-01 | not significant |
| 20 m sprint (s) | 3.081 | 3.021 | -0.060 | 1.232e-01 | not significant |
| Sleep quality (1-10) | 6.21 | 6.85 | +0.64 | 7.851e-02 | not significant |

Every marker moved in the direction favouring immersion, but only muscle soreness
separated the two groups at the 0.05 level. Creatine kinase came close (p = 0.053) with
a mean drop of 67 U/L, roughly 16% below passive rest.

## Practical recommendation

The reliable effect here is on how players feel, not on what they can do. Soreness fell
by 10 points on a 100-point scale, which players notice. Jump height, sprint time, and
the blood marker of muscle damage all leaned the same way, but none of them separated
cleanly at this sample size, and the performance differences are small in absolute terms:
1.8 cm of jump height and 0.06 s over 20 m.

For a congested fixture list, where a player has to be back on the pitch inside three
days and perceived soreness drives whether he trains, post-match immersion is a
reasonable and cheap thing to offer. It should not be sold as a performance restorer on
this evidence, and it is worth remembering that regular cold-water exposure after
training is thought to blunt some adaptation to strength work. The pragmatic split is to
use it in fixture congestion and to leave it out during blocks aimed at building
strength.

Two caveats limit how far this goes. Each player appears once, so between-player
variation carries the whole comparison, and the passive control means players knew which
condition they were in, which is hard to avoid with a cold bath but does load the
soreness rating, the one outcome that reached significance.
