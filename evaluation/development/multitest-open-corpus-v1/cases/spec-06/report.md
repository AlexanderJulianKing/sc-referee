# Resistance training in adults aged 65 to 80: week 12 outcomes

Sixty-four community-dwelling adults were randomised to twelve weeks of supervised
progressive resistance training (n = 32) or to a stretching-only control (n = 32).
Assessors blind to allocation measured five outcomes at week 12, and those five are
treated here as one family: leg press one-repetition maximum, four-metre gait speed,
five-times sit-to-stand time, appendicular lean mass, and the SF-36 physical function
score.

The family-wide error rate was fixed at 5% before any test was run. With five outcomes
in the family, the Sidak per-outcome threshold is

    1 - (1 - 0.05) ^ (1/5) = 1 - 0.95 ^ 0.2 = 1 - 0.989794 = 0.010206

so each raw p-value is judged against 0.010206 rather than against 0.05. Group means
were compared with Welch two-sample t-tests.

| Outcome | Stretch | Resistance | p | Against 0.010206 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Leg press 1RM (kg) | 83.33 | 107.26 | 0.00013 | clears |
| 4 m gait speed (m/s) | 0.98 | 1.10 | 0.00988 | clears |
| 5x sit-to-stand (s) | 12.66 | 10.49 | 0.00348 | clears |
| Appendicular lean mass (kg) | 19.22 | 20.41 | 0.17849 | does not clear |
| SF-36 physical function | 66.88 | 74.41 | 0.03197 | does not clear |

Three of the five outcomes clear the threshold: leg press strength, gait speed and
sit-to-stand time. The strength gain is the largest single effect, at roughly 24 kg.
Lean mass moved by about 1.2 kg and is nowhere near the threshold, which fits the usual
picture of twelve-week trials in this age group, where neural and functional gains run
ahead of tissue accrual. The SF-36 physical function difference of 7.5 points would have
been called significant at an uncorrected 5% level, but it does not survive the
family-wide control, and we do not claim it.

The Bonferroni threshold for the same family, 0.05 / 5 = 0.01, is very slightly stricter
than the Sidak value of 0.010206, and here the two rules reach the same three
conclusions.
