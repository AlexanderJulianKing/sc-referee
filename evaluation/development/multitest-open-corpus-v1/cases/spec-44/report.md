# Hypotonic versus isotonic oral rehydration in calf scours

## Setting

86 dairy calves presenting with diarrhoea on cooperating farms over two calving seasons,
43 given the standard isotonic oral rehydration solution and 43 given the hypotonic
product. Each calf contributes one record. The outcome family is five measures: time to
normal faecal consistency, venous blood pH at 24 h, base excess at 24 h, body weight
change over 72 h, and serum sodium at 24 h.

## Assumption checks and test choice

For each outcome, normality was checked within each solution group with Shapiro-Wilk and
equality of variances with Levene's test on medians, both read at the five percent level.

| Outcome | Shapiro isotonic | Shapiro hypotonic | Levene | Test used |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Time to resolution (h) | 0.118 | 0.0004 | 0.899 | Mann-Whitney U |
| Blood pH at 24 h | 0.085 | 0.471 | 0.102 | Student t |
| Base excess (mmol/L) | 0.995 | 0.956 | 0.847 | Student t |
| Weight change (%) | 0.864 | 0.875 | 0.258 | Student t |
| Serum sodium (mmol/L) | 0.945 | 0.881 | 0.867 | Student t |

Only time to resolution failed a check. Recovery times are right-skewed, with a tail of
calves taking far longer than the rest, and the hypotonic group's Shapiro p-value of
0.0004 reflects that. Since the mean is not a good summary of a skewed distribution, that
outcome was tested with the rank-based Mann-Whitney U. The other four passed both checks
and were compared with the equal-variance two-sample t-test.

## Family-wide correction and results

The five p-values, whichever test produced them, were treated as one family and corrected
together with Holm's step-down procedure at a family-wide five percent level. A difference
is declared only where the adjusted value clears that level.

| Outcome | Isotonic | Hypotonic | Raw p | Adjusted p | Conclusion |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Time to resolution (h) | 59.6 | 51.3 | 0.0236 | 0.0473 | difference |
| Blood pH at 24 h | 7.27 | 7.34 | 0.0001 | 0.0006 | difference |
| Base excess (mmol/L) | -4.90 | -2.57 | 0.0027 | 0.0082 | difference |
| Weight change over 72 h (%) | -1.51 | -0.03 | 0.0013 | 0.0054 | difference |
| Serum sodium (mmol/L) | 139.6 | 137.4 | 0.0316 | 0.0473 | difference |

All five survive correction, though time to resolution and serum sodium are close to the
line at an adjusted 0.0473 and would not survive a stricter family level.

## Conclusion

Calves on the hypotonic solution reached normal faecal consistency about eight hours
sooner, corrected their acidosis better by 24 h (higher pH, base excess about 2.3 mmol/L
less negative), and held their body weight over 72 h instead of losing roughly 1.5
percent. Serum sodium was about 2 mmol/L lower on the hypotonic product, which is the
expected consequence of the lower sodium content and stays inside the normal range for
calves here, but it is the outcome to watch in animals that are already hyponatraemic on
presentation. For uncomplicated calf scours on these farms the hypotonic solution is the
better choice.
