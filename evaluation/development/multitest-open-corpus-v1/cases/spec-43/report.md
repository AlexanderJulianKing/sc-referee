# Replacing pharmacological zinc oxide in weaner diets

## Setting

One weaning batch of 100 pigs, 50 on the zinc oxide control diet and 50 on a blend of
organic acids and essential oils, followed for 35 days post-weaning. Six outcomes were
measured. Final weight, average daily gain and feed conversion were pre-specified as the
performance family, since those three carry the commercial decision. Diarrhoea score,
faecal zinc and jejunal villus height were recorded as supporting measures.

## Analysis

All six outcomes were compared between diets with a two-sample t-test for a difference in
means. The three performance outcomes were corrected together with Holm's procedure at a
five percent family level, and the values shown for them below are the Holm-adjusted ones.
The three supporting measures were read against the plain five percent level.

## Results

| Outcome | Zinc oxide | Acid blend | p | Result |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Final weight (kg) | 21.48 | 20.70 | 0.1918 | not significant |
| Average daily gain (g/d) | 381.3 | 356.5 | 0.1071 | not significant |
| Feed conversion (feed:gain) | 1.51 | 1.60 | 0.0254 | significant |
| Diarrhoea score (0-3) | 0.89 | 1.12 | 0.0197 | significant |
| Faecal zinc (mg/kg DM) | 2414 | 285 | <0.0001 | significant |
| Jejunal villus height (um) | 412.8 | 437.0 | 0.0822 | not significant |

The first three rows are the corrected performance family; the last three are
uncorrected. Before correction the performance p-values were 0.1918 for final weight,
0.0535 for daily gain and 0.0085 for feed conversion, so Holm changed the daily gain
result from borderline to clearly non-significant and left the other two verdicts alone.

## Conclusion

Pigs on the acid blend finished 0.78 kg lighter and gained about 25 g/day less, but
neither gap holds up once the performance family is corrected. Feed conversion is the one
performance outcome that survives: the acid blend needed about 0.09 more feed per unit of
gain, which is a real cost at batch scale. On the supporting side the acid blend produced
a higher diarrhoea score and, as expected, a large drop in faecal zinc, the whole point of
removing pharmacological zinc oxide. Villus height was slightly higher on the acid blend
but not significantly so.

The acid blend is a workable replacement on growth grounds, since final weight and daily
gain are not distinguishable after correction, but it is not free: worse feed conversion
and more loose faeces. Whether that trade is acceptable depends on how the unit values the
roughly eightfold reduction in zinc excretion against a modest feed efficiency penalty.
One weaning batch on one site is a thin basis for a permanent diet change, and the feed
conversion penalty should be confirmed on a second batch before the control diet is
dropped.
