# Ocean acidification and juvenile Pacific oysters

## Exposure

One hundred juvenile *Magallana gigas* were reared for ten weeks in flow-through
seawater held either at present-day pH (ambient, 50 animals) or at a lowered pH
matching an end-of-century projection (50 animals). Temperature, salinity, feed ration,
and stocking density were matched between the two systems. Each oyster was measured
once at the end of the exposure on six outcomes: shell length, shell thickness, dry
tissue mass, net calcification rate, respiration, and days survived out of 70.

## Whole-panel screen

Before looking at any single outcome we asked whether the panel as a whole responded to
pH. For each outcome we took the difference in group means divided by the pooled
standard deviation, squared it, and summed the six values into one statistic. That sum
was 2.720. Its null distribution was built by reshuffling the treatment labels across
the 100 oysters 2000 times and recomputing the same sum, which gives a panel p-value of
0.0005. The gate is therefore open at the five-percent level, and only because of that
did we go on to the individual outcomes. Had the panel statistic not been extreme, this
report would have stopped at the previous sentence with no per-outcome verdicts, since
sifting six outcomes for whichever ones look significant is exactly the habit the gate
exists to block.

## Individual outcomes

| Outcome | Ambient | Low pH | p | Verdict |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Shell thickness (um) | 409.2 | 349.0 | 2.8e-05 | significant |
| Calcification (mg/day) | 1.40 | 1.12 | 1.6e-04 | significant |
| Survival (days of 70) | 68.2 | 64.4 | 2.6e-05 | significant |
| Respiration (umol/h) | 3.67 | 4.16 | 0.0052 | significant |
| Shell length (mm) | 23.91 | 22.50 | 0.051 | not significant |
| Dry tissue mass (mg) | 95.13 | 92.63 | 0.52 | not significant |

The clearest signal is in the shell. Thickness fell by 60 um, about 15 percent, and net
calcification fell by 0.28 mg/day, a fifth of the ambient rate. Respiration went the
other way, rising by half a umol/h, which is the pattern expected when animals spend
more energy on acid-base regulation. Survival was 3.8 days shorter on average out of a
possible 70, and the low-pH group was also far more variable, so the mean understates
what happened to the worst-affected animals.

Soft tissue was largely spared. Dry tissue mass differed by 2.5 mg (p = 0.52), and
shell length sits right on the line at p = 0.051 with a 1.4 mm difference. We would not
call shell length a null result on that evidence; it is a difference in the same
direction as everything else that this sample size cannot resolve. Taken together the
picture is of animals that keep growing in outline while building a thinner, more
weakly mineralised shell and paying more metabolically to do it, which is the
combination most likely to matter for predation and handling survival in a hatchery
setting.
