# Two injectable anaesthetic protocols for feline ovariohysterectomy

Ninety-two cats presented for routine ovariohysterectomy over one clinic year were anaesthetised
with one of two injectable protocols, allocated by alternating theatre days (46 cats each). Six
outcomes were recorded: time to loss of righting reflex, time to sternal recumbency, lowest mean
arterial pressure, lowest rectal temperature, pain score at two hours, and apnoea events during
maintenance. All six were compared between protocols with a Welch two-sample test.

Lowest arterial pressure and apnoea events were designated the safety outcomes before the data
were analysed. Those two were passed through a Holm correction at a five-percent family level,
and the table gives their adjusted p-values. The four efficacy outcomes are shown with the
p-values as the tests produced them.

| Outcome | Protocol A | Protocol B | Difference | p | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Induction time (s) | 88.0 | 72.0 | -16.0 | 0.0008 | significant |
| Recovery time (min) | 46.0 | 58.0 | +12.0 | 0.0008 | significant |
| Lowest MAP (mmHg) | 67.9 | 62.0 | -6.0 | 0.0109 | significant |
| Lowest temperature (C) | 36.80 | 36.40 | -0.40 | 0.0046 | significant |
| Pain score at 2 h (0 to 20) | 5.17 | 4.11 | -1.07 | 0.0426 | significant |
| Apnoea events | 0.57 | 1.07 | +0.50 | 0.0271 | significant |

Differences are protocol B minus protocol A. The two safety rows carry Holm-adjusted values;
their raw p-values were 0.0055 for lowest MAP and 0.0271 for apnoea events. Apnoea events sit at
the top of the two-test Holm ladder, so its adjusted value equals its raw value.

## Recommendation

Protocol B gives the smoother start. Cats go down about 16 seconds faster and score about one
point lower on the two-hour pain scale, which matters for a high-throughput neutering list.
Everything after the incision favours protocol A. Recovery is 12 minutes longer under B, cats are
0.4 C colder at their lowest reading, mean arterial pressure runs about 6 mmHg lower, and apnoea
events roughly double, from 0.57 to 1.07 per cat. Both pre-designated safety outcomes therefore
run against protocol B, and both survive their family-wide correction.

For routine feline ovariohysterectomy in this hospital we recommend protocol A. Protocol B stays
a reasonable option where induction speed is the binding constraint, provided capnography and
active warming are in place and a recovery bay is free for the longer wake-up.

Two limits are worth stating. Allocation was by alternating theatre day rather than by cat, so
anything that tracks day of the week travels with the protocol. And the pressure and temperature
readings are single lowest values per cat, not areas under a curve, so they say how far each cat
dipped but not for how long.
