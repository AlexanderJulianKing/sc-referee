# Disposable and powered respirators in a stone-cutting workshop

Fifty-eight masons in one workshop wore either a standard disposable respirator (29 men) or a
powered air-purifying respirator (29 men) for twelve weeks. Exposure and respiratory endpoints
were measured at the end of the period. Each endpoint was tested on its own with a Welch
two-sample test and its p-value compared with 0.05.

| # | Endpoint | Disposable | Powered | p | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Respirable silica behind mask (mg/m3) | 0.0418 | 0.0190 | 3.6e-07 | significant |
| 2 | Quantitative fit factor | 41.9 | 168.0 | 1.7e-13 | significant |
| 3 | FEV1 (% predicted) | 94.2 | 96.8 | 0.241 | not significant |
| 4 | Chronic cough score (0 to 12) | 4.07 | 3.17 | 0.115 | not significant |
| 5 | Comfort (0 to 10) | 6.45 | 5.45 | 0.041 | significant |
| 6 | Wear compliance (% of exposed time) | 77.3 | 87.4 | 0.0015 | significant |

## Reading the endpoints

The two exposure endpoints move together and move hard. Behind-mask silica is 55 percent lower
with the powered units, and the fit factor is four times higher, which is the mechanism: a
positive-pressure hood does not depend on a face seal that a beard, a jaw movement, or a poorly
sized cup can break. Wear compliance is 10 percentage points higher as well, so the powered units
are not only better when worn, they are worn more of the time.

The two health endpoints did not separate the groups. FEV1 differs by 2.6 percentage points of
predicted and cough score by 0.9 points, both in the direction that favours the powered units,
neither established by these data. That is the expected result at this sample size over twelve
weeks. Silicosis develops over years, so lung function here is better read as a baseline for
follow-up than as an outcome this study could move.

Comfort runs the other way. Masons rated the powered units one point lower on the ten-point
scale, which matches the usual complaints about hood weight, battery packs, and heat.

## Recommended policy

Move the workshop to powered air-purifying respirators for all cutting and dressing work. The
exposure case is strong and the compliance figure says the men will keep them on despite rating
them less comfortable. Two things should go with the change: a battery and filter maintenance
schedule, since a powered unit with a flat battery is worse than a disposable one, and repeat fit
testing at six months. Keep disposable respirators for short incidental tasks away from the saws.
