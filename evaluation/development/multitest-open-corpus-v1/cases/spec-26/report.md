# Drip versus furrow irrigation in cotton

One season, 72 harvest quadrats of 10 m2 on adjacent fields of the same soil series and
cultivar, 36 under subsurface drip and 36 under traditional furrow irrigation. Each
outcome was compared between the two systems with a Welch two-sample t-test.

## Multiplicity

Six agronomic outcomes were measured on the same quadrats and are reported together, so
they are treated as one family of six p-values. The family was corrected in a single
step with the Benjamini-Hochberg step-up procedure, which controls the false discovery
rate at q = 0.05 across all six outcomes at once. No outcome is called significant on
its raw p-value.

## Results

| Outcome | Furrow | Drip | Raw p | BH adjusted p | Decision |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Lint yield (kg/ha) | 1428.2 | 1610.7 | 0.0015 | 0.0029 | survives |
| Water applied (mm) | 775.1 | 548.0 | 2.3e-28 | 1.4e-27 | survives |
| Water use efficiency (kg/ha/mm) | 1.85 | 2.98 | 1.9e-14 | 5.8e-14 | survives |
| Fibre length (mm) | 28.74 | 28.97 | 0.52 | 0.63 | not supported |
| Micronaire | 4.30 | 4.30 | 0.95 | 0.95 | not supported |
| Soil salinity (dS/m) | 1.71 | 2.09 | 0.0036 | 0.0054 | survives |

## Interpretation

Four of the six differences survive the correction. Drip used about 227 mm less water
per season and still produced about 182 kg/ha more lint, so water use efficiency rose
from 1.85 to 2.98 kg of lint per hectare-millimetre, a gain of roughly 60 percent. Those
three results are one result seen from three sides, and they are the case for the
capital cost of a subsurface drip system in this district: the same or better lint on
close to two-thirds of the water.

Fibre quality does not separate the systems. Neither upper half mean length nor
micronaire shows a difference that survives correction, and the raw p-values for both
are large enough that the season gives no hint of one. Growers switching systems should
not expect a quality premium or penalty from irrigation method alone.

The salinity result runs the other way and should temper the investment case. End of
season soil salinity was higher under drip, 2.09 against 1.71 dS/m, and that difference
survives correction. Drip wets a smaller soil volume and applies less leaching water, so
salts accumulate in the root zone. Over one season the levels stay below the point where
upland cotton loses yield, but a drip programme here needs a leaching plan and repeat
salinity monitoring rather than an assumption that the first season's water saving
carries forward unchanged.
