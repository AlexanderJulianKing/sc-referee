# Portable HEPA filtration in primary school classrooms

## Design

Half the classrooms in one primary school were fitted with portable HEPA filtration
units for a full term; the other half were left unchanged. Classrooms were monitored
for four weeks. Pupils in the study cohort gave weekly symptom and lung-function
readings, which were reduced to one summary row per pupil for analysis: 84 pupils, 42
in filtered rooms and 42 in unfiltered rooms. Each of the five outcomes was compared
between room types with Welch's two-sample t-test and judged against a five percent
cutoff.

## Exposure

Filtration did what it was installed to do. Mean personal PM2.5 exposure was 17.11
micrograms per cubic metre in unfiltered rooms and 11.84 in filtered rooms, a drop of
5.27 (about 31 percent), p = 1.6e-05. This is the strongest result in the study and the
one least dependent on pupil self-report.

## Health outcomes

| Outcome | Unfiltered | Filtered | p | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| pm25_exposure_ug_m3 | 17.11 | 11.84 | 1.6e-05 | significant |
| peak_flow_l_min | 253.62 | 253.02 | 0.953 | not significant |
| cough_days | 5.90 | 4.74 | 0.143 | not significant |
| absence_days | 2.69 | 1.98 | 0.087 | not significant |
| feno_ppb | 21.89 | 17.15 | 0.0095 | significant |

Two of the five outcomes were significant: PM2.5 exposure and fractional exhaled nitric
oxide. FeNO, a marker of airway inflammation, was 4.7 ppb lower in filtered rooms. Peak
expiratory flow was flat, which is not surprising over four weeks in a general cohort
rather than an asthmatic one. Cough days and absence days both fell by roughly a fifth
but neither reached the cutoff at this cohort size.

## Recommendation

The exposure reduction is solid and the FeNO drop gives it a plausible biological
follow-through, so wider installation is worth doing. Two qualifications belong with
that recommendation. First, this is a single-school comparison with rooms, not pupils,
as the thing that was assigned, so pupils in the same room share whatever else that
room does; the pupil-level test treats them as independent and will read as more
precise than the design supports. Second, five outcomes were each tested at five
percent with no adjustment for the number of tests, which raises the chance that at
least one of the two significant results is a false alarm to roughly one in four. FeNO
is the outcome to name in advance and power properly in any expansion, and the next
round should also record which pupils have diagnosed asthma, since that is the group
where a peak flow benefit would show up if there is one.
