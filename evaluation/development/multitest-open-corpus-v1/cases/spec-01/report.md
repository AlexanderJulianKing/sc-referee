# Rumen-protected methionine under summer heat stress

## Design

Sixty lactating Holstein cows on a single farm were put on either the herd control
ration or the same ration plus a rumen-protected methionine supplement, 30 cows per
group, for six weeks of summer. Milk and blood measures were taken during the final
week; each cow contributes one row. The comparison is between groups, one measure at a
time, using Welch's two-sample t-test (unequal variances).

## Outcomes and group means

Outcomes in the order they were measured and analysed:

| # | Outcome | Control | Methionine | Difference | p |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | milk_yield_kg_d | 28.79 | 30.33 | +1.54 | 0.165 |
| 2 | milk_fat_pct | 3.585 | 3.704 | +0.120 | 0.176 |
| 3 | milk_protein_pct | 3.042 | 3.178 | +0.135 | 0.021 |
| 4 | somatic_cell_score | 4.13 | 3.69 | -0.43 | 0.116 |
| 5 | rectal_temp_c | 39.37 | 39.28 | -0.09 | 0.343 |
| 6 | plasma_nefa_mmol_l | 0.219 | 0.180 | -0.040 | 0.068 |

Each outcome was judged on its own against a five percent cutoff. On that basis one
outcome came out significant: **milk true protein percent** (p = 0.021), higher on the
supplement by 0.135 percentage points. The other five did not clear the cutoff. Plasma
NEFA (p = 0.068) and somatic cell score (p = 0.116) both moved in the direction the
supplement is supposed to move them, but neither difference is separable from noise at
this group size. Afternoon rectal temperature was essentially flat between groups.

## What this suggests for summer rations

The protein response is the result to build on. A 0.135 point lift in true protein at
roughly 30 kg of milk a day is about 40 g of extra milk protein per cow per day, which
is worth having on a component-priced contract and fits methionine being the first
limiting amino acid in a hot-weather ration where dry matter intake sags. Supplementing
methionine in the summer ration is defensible on component yield alone, and does not
need the thermoregulation claim to justify it.

Two cautions before this goes into a formulation recommendation. Rectal temperature did
not budge, so nothing here supports selling the supplement as heat relief. And with six
outcomes each judged at five percent, roughly one study in four like this would turn up
at least one significant result even if the supplement did nothing, so a single
significant outcome out of six is thin ground for a firm claim. A confirmation run on a
second farm, with milk protein named in advance as the outcome of interest, would
settle it.
