# Telehealth follow-up after total hip replacement

Half of one year's elective total hip replacement patients were offered a telehealth
follow-up pathway and half the usual in-person clinic pathway, 65 patients each. Six
outcomes were collected at the twelve-week review. Each outcome was compared between
pathways with a Welch two-sample t-test, and each printed line carries its own verdict
wording, chosen as the line is built according to whether that outcome's p-value sits
under 0.05.

## Results at twelve weeks

| Outcome | In-person | Telehealth | Difference | p | Verdict wording in the script |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Oxford Hip Score (0-48) | 38.20 | 37.78 | -0.42 | 0.6907 | no significant difference |
| Pain VAS (0-100) | 18.95 | 20.57 | +1.62 | 0.4181 | no significant difference |
| Physiotherapy sessions | 9.23 | 7.63 | -1.60 | 0.003016 | statistically significant |
| Readmission days | 0.40 | 0.55 | +0.15 | 0.4924 | no significant difference |
| Travel cost (GBP) | 84.04 | 23.14 | -60.91 | 6.037e-20 | statistically significant |
| Satisfaction (1-10) | 8.34 | 8.54 | +0.20 | 0.3364 | no significant difference |

## Should telehealth become the standard pathway?

The clinical picture at twelve weeks is one of similarity. Oxford Hip Score, pain,
readmission days, and satisfaction all came out close, with differences well inside what
this sample can resolve. The two outcomes that separated are the ones a change of
delivery channel would be expected to touch directly.

Travel cost is the clear win: GBP 84 down to GBP 23 over twelve weeks, a saving of about
GBP 61 per patient that falls on the patient's own pocket and, for people who cannot
drive after surgery, on whoever takes them to clinic.

Physiotherapy attendance is the warning. Telehealth patients attended 1.6 fewer sessions,
9.2 down to 7.6, and that difference is not explained by chance. Rehabilitation adherence
is the mechanism through which hip replacement outcomes are usually thought to be won or
lost, so a drop in attendance is worth taking seriously even though the twelve-week
function scores look unchanged. Twelve weeks may simply be too early to see the
consequence.

My recommendation is to offer telehealth as the default follow-up pathway while treating
physiotherapy attendance as a monitored condition of doing so, not to switch wholesale
and stop looking. Concretely: keep the pathway, but add an attendance check at six weeks
that triggers an in-person physiotherapy appointment for anyone falling behind, and
re-examine hip scores at one year before calling the two pathways equivalent. Patients
who are frail, live alone, or had a complicated recovery should keep the clinic pathway
by default.

Two limits on the above. Allocation was not randomised within the year's list, so
unmeasured differences between the two halves could carry some of the effect. And these
are twelve-week outcomes only; the durability question is still open.
