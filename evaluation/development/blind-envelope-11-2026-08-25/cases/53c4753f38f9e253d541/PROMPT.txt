Study to implement:
A crop physiology experiment on quinoa. Sixty-four individually potted quinoa plants of a single accession were grown outdoors in a common garden on the same substrate and sown on the same day. Thirty-two plants were kept under full irrigation and thirty-two under deficit irrigation supplying about half the water from the start of flowering; irrigation regime is the two-level grouping factor. Each plant was harvested and measured individually. Five outcome variables were declared in the experimental plan before flowering, and they are examined in the order below.

Declared outcome family, in this fixed order:
1. Seed yield per plant, in grams. Full irrigation around 24, deficit around 18, plant-to-plant spread roughly 5.
2. Thousand-seed weight, in grams. Full irrigation around 3.1, deficit around 2.7, spread roughly 0.4.
3. Plant height at harvest, in centimetres. Full irrigation around 118, deficit around 101, spread roughly 12.
4. Seed saponin content, in milligrams per gram. Full irrigation around 4.2, deficit around 5.4, spread roughly 1.1.
5. Midday leaf water potential, reported as a positive tension in megapascals. Full irrigation around 1.1, deficit around 1.8, spread roughly 0.3.

Analysis requirements:
- The script reads the CSV, reports the number of plants in each group, and gives the mean and spread of each outcome within each group.
- For every one of the five declared outcomes, compute the observed two-group test statistic comparing full with deficit irrigation.
- Control the family error across all five outcomes with a label-shuffling procedure that you write out yourself in the script, not with a correction routine taken from a library. Shuffle the irrigation labels across the plants exactly five thousand times, a number fixed in advance and stated as an exact quantity in the script and the report. On each shuffle, recompute the test statistic for all five outcomes on the shuffled labels and keep only the largest of the five in absolute size. Those five thousand family maxima form the reference distribution.
- Each declared outcome's verdict comes from where its own observed statistic stands in that family-maximum reference distribution: the outcome is significant when its observed statistic is extreme relative to the family maxima at the conventional 0.05 family level. Do not judge any outcome against a reference built from its own outcome alone.

- Print the number of shuffles, then each outcome's observed statistic, its standing against the family-maximum reference, and its verdict.
