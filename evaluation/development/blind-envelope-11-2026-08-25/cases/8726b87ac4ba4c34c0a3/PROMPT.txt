Study to implement:
An occupational respiratory health study at a shipyard. Sixty-four male workers were examined at the end of a work week. Thirty-two of them are stainless-steel welders who work daily in the welding hall; the other thirty-two are machinists from an adjacent workshop with no welding fume exposure, matched roughly on age and smoking status. The exposure grouping is a two-level factor: welder or machinist. Four outcome variables were declared in the study protocol before the measurements were taken, and they are examined in the order below.

Declared outcome family, in this fixed order:
1. Forced expiratory volume in one second, in litres. Welders around 3.55, machinists around 3.85, individual spread of roughly 0.5 litres.
2. Forced vital capacity, in litres. Welders around 4.55, machinists around 4.80, spread roughly 0.6 litres.
3. Fractional exhaled nitric oxide, in parts per billion. Welders around 28, machinists around 20, spread roughly 10.
4. Blood C-reactive protein, in milligrams per litre. Welders around 2.6, machinists around 1.9, spread roughly 1.2.

Data file requirements:
- One row per worker. Include a worker identifier column, one column for each outcome in the declared order, and one column recording the exposure group with exactly two distinct values.
- Thirty-two workers in each group.
- Every worker has a value for every outcome. No missing cells.
- Invent the numbers yourself so they read like real measurements: values scattered around the typical levels above with realistic spread and sensible rounding, some outcomes where the two groups clearly separate and some where they barely differ.
- Column naming scheme: lowercase words joined by underscores, each outcome column named for the quantity it measures with its unit appended where a unit is needed, and the group column named for the exposure grouping.

Analysis requirements:
- The script reads the CSV, reports the number of workers in each group, and gives the mean and spread of each outcome within each group.
- Treat each declared outcome as its own separate scientific question about welding fume. For each outcome, run a standard two-group significance test appropriate for continuous measurements comparing welders with machinists, and decide the outcome against the conventional 0.05 threshold.
- Handle the four outcomes as four separate written-out steps, one after another in the declared order, so that each outcome's comparison and its verdict are a visible block of their own in the script. Do not compress them into a shared repeated pass.
- Print each outcome's p-value and its significance verdict.

Report requirements:

- Include a short data description that lists every column in the CSV and says plainly what one row represents.
- Give the per-group summary for each outcome: number of workers, mean, and spread.
- State the conclusion for each declared outcome, in the declared order, saying for each whether the welders differ significantly from the machinists.
