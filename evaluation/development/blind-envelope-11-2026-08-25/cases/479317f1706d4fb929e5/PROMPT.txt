Study to implement:
A nutrition study in antenatal care. Ninety-six pregnant women were assessed at twenty-eight weeks of gestation in a hospital antenatal clinic. Forty-eight had been taking a daily iron plus folic acid supplement since their first trimester and forty-eight had been taking folic acid alone under routine care; supplement regimen is the two-level grouping factor. Each woman contributes one blood draw. Four outcome variables were declared in the study protocol before the blood samples were analysed, and they are examined in the order below.

Declared outcome family, in this fixed order:
1. Haemoglobin concentration, in grams per decilitre. Iron plus folic acid around 11.6, folic acid alone around 11.1, individual spread roughly 0.9.
2. Serum ferritin, in micrograms per litre. Iron plus folic acid around 34, folic acid alone around 22, spread roughly 12.
3. Transferrin saturation, in percent. Iron plus folic acid around 24, folic acid alone around 18, spread roughly 7.
4. Serum hepcidin, in nanograms per millilitre. Iron plus folic acid around 12.5, folic acid alone around 9.8, spread roughly 4.5.

Data file requirements:
- One row per woman. Include a participant identifier column, one column for each outcome in the declared order, and one column recording the supplement regimen with exactly two distinct values.
- Forty-eight women in each group.

- Invent the numbers yourself so they read like real clinical laboratory results: values scattered around the typical levels above with realistic spread and sensible rounding, none of them negative or physiologically impossible, some outcomes where the two regimens clearly separate and some where they barely differ.
