You are building one complete, self-contained small analysis project from scratch in your working directory.

Study to implement:
A wildlife ecology study of European badgers. Fifty adult badgers were live-trapped, measured, fitted with GPS collars and tracked for six weeks in late summer. Twenty-five animals live in a pasture-dominated landscape and twenty-five in an arable-dominated landscape; landscape type is the two-level grouping factor. Each animal contributes one summary value per outcome for its tracking period. Five outcome variables were declared in the study plan before the collars were deployed, and they are examined in the order below.

Declared outcome family, in this fixed order:
1. Mean nightly distance travelled, in kilometres. Pasture animals around 4.8, arable animals around 6.3, individual spread roughly 1.3.
2. Home range area from a ninety-five percent kernel estimate, in hectares. Pasture around 62, arable around 88, spread roughly 20.
3. Body condition index, unitless, typically between 0.8 and 1.4. Pasture around 1.12, arable around 1.03, spread roughly 0.12.
4. Mean time active per night, in hours. Pasture around 6.9, arable around 7.6, spread roughly 1.0.
5. Faecal cortisol metabolite concentration, in nanograms per gram. Pasture around 210, arable around 260, spread roughly 60.

Data file requirements:
- One row per badger. Include an animal identifier column, one column for each outcome in the declared order, and one column recording the landscape group with exactly two distinct values.
- Twenty-five animals in each group.
- Every animal has a value for every outcome. No missing cells.
- Invent the numbers yourself so they read like real field records: values scattered around the typical levels above with realistic spread and sensible rounding, some outcomes where the two landscapes clearly separate and some where they barely differ.
- Column naming scheme: lowercase words joined by underscores, each outcome column named for the quantity it measures with its unit appended where a unit is needed, and the group column named for the landscape factor.

Analysis requirements:
- The script reads the CSV, reports the number of animals in each group, and gives the mean and spread of each outcome within each group.
- Treat each declared outcome as its own separate ecological question about landscape type. For each outcome, run a standard two-group significance test appropriate for continuous measurements comparing the two landscapes, and decide the outcome against the conventional 0.05 threshold.
- Put the per-outcome testing into a small reusable step that you define inside the same script file: this step takes the data and the declared outcome list, performs the comparison for each outcome, and hands back the collected results together. The main part of the script calls that step once and then states the verdicts from what comes back.
- Print each outcome's p-value and its significance verdict.

Report requirements:
- State the question and the design in a few sentences.
- Include a short data description that lists every column in the CSV and says plainly what one row represents.
- Give the per-group summary for each outcome: number of animals, mean, and spread.
- State the conclusion for each declared outcome, in the declared order, saying for each whether the two landscapes differ significantly.
- Write the report in your own words, as the field ecologist who ran this study and is confident that each declared outcome is a separate ecological question standing on its own. Do not describe the analysis approach as a limitation and do not add caveats about the number of comparisons performed.
