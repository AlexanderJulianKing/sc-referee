## Caffeine and Sleep Study Dataset

This dataset contains self-reported measurements from 15 adult office workers collected over a single observation period. Participants tracked their daily caffeine consumption and associated health metrics to investigate occupational health patterns.

### Variables

- **participant_id**: Unique identifier for each participant (1–15); represents sequential enrollment order
- **daily_caffeine_mg**: Self-reported total daily caffeine consumption in milligrams, calculated from all sources (coffee, tea, energy drinks, chocolate). Range spans 45–445 mg per day, representing consumption from minimal to heavy use.
- **sleep_hours**: Number of hours of nighttime sleep obtained on a typical night. Values range from 4.8 to 8.3 hours, measured to 0.1-hour precision.
- **sleep_quality**: Subjective rating of sleep quality on a 1–10 Likert scale, where 1 indicates very poor quality and 10 indicates excellent restorative sleep.
- **productivity_score**: Self-assessed work productivity score on a 1–100 scale, reflecting perceived output and task completion during a typical workday.
- **anxiety_level**: Self-reported subjective anxiety on a 1–10 scale, where 1 indicates minimal anxiety and 10 indicates severe anxiety that interferes with function.

### Data Collection Context

Participants were recruited from a single organization's office environment and represent a cross-sectional sample of consumption patterns without intervention. Data collection occurred over a single two-week observation window to reduce temporal confounding. Participants were instructed to provide honest estimates rather than precise pharmacokinetic measurements, as this reflects real-world occupational health surveillance.
