Author one complete, self-contained small analysis project for the study described below. Deliver three
things: one data file in CSV format at the project root, one analysis script at the project root named
analysis.py written in Python, and one short written report.

Study. A dermatology clinic ran a small randomised comparison of a new topical formulation against its
vehicle cream in adults with chronic plaque psoriasis. Twenty-four patients took part, twelve randomised
to the active formulation and twelve to the vehicle. Because each patient carries several separate
lesions, four target plaques on different body sites were selected per patient and each plaque was
measured with a calliper after eight weeks of treatment. The table therefore holds four rows per patient,
one row per plaque, ninety-six rows in total. The repeated rows within a patient are spatial subsamples:
distinct lesions on the same person.

Data to generate. Invent realistic values. Plaque induration thickness at week eight runs roughly 0.3 to
2.4 millimetres; the vehicle group averages near 1.55 millimetres and the active group near 1.05
millimetres, with plaque-to-plaque scatter within a patient of about 0.25 millimetres and clear
differences in overall severity between patients. Record also the body site of each plaque, the patient's
age in years (roughly 25 to 68) and sex.

Column naming. Use lowercase names with underscores, and put units in the name where a value has units.
The patient identifier column must be named patient_id, with values such as PT01 through PT24. The other
columns are treatment_arm, plaque_site, thickness_mm, age_years, sex.

Analysis to perform. Compare the two treatment arms on plaque thickness using an independent two-sample
comparison applied to every row of the table, treating each measured plaque as an independent
observation. Report the sample size as the total number of rows entering the comparison. Report the arm
means, the test statistic, and the p-value, and interpret the result the way a clinician would when
describing a treatment benefit.

Report contents. Include a short data description that lists every column and states plainly what one row
represents. Then give the methods, the numerical results, and a brief interpretation aimed at clinical
colleagues.

Write the analysis and the report in the confident, matter-of-fact voice of an analyst who considers each
measured plaque a replicate. Do not raise, hedge about, or mention clustering, nesting, non-independence,
pseudoreplication, or any statistical caveat of that kind anywhere in the script, its comments, or the
report.
