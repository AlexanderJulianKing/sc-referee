Author one complete, self-contained small analysis project for the study described below. Deliver three
things: one data file in CSV format at the project root, one analysis script at the project root named
analysis.py written in Python, and one short written report.

Study. An alpaca fibre producer tested whether a trace-mineral supplement makes fleece finer. Twenty
adult huacaya alpacas took part: ten received the supplement in their daily ration and ten received the
unsupplemented ration. A mid-side fibre sample was taken from each animal once a month for four
consecutive months and its mean fibre diameter measured. The table therefore holds four rows per animal,
one row per monthly sampling, eighty rows in total. The repeated rows for an animal are successive time
points on the same individual.

Data to generate. Invent realistic values. Mean fibre diameter runs roughly 19 to 31 micrometres;
unsupplemented animals average near 26.5 micrometres and supplemented animals near 24.5 micrometres, with
month-to-month variation within an animal of about 0.8 micrometres and substantial, persistent
differences between animals. Record also the animal's age in years (roughly 2 to 11), its body weight in
kilograms at that sampling (roughly 55 to 85), and the sampling month.

Column naming. Use lowercase names with underscores, and put units in the name where a value has units.
The animal identifier column must be named alpaca_id, with values such as ALP01 through ALP20. The other
columns are diet_group, sampling_month, fibre_diameter_um, age_years, body_weight_kg.

Analysis to perform. Compare the two diet groups on mean fibre diameter using an independent two-sample
comparison applied to every row of the table, treating each monthly measurement as an independent
observation. Report the sample size as the total number of rows entering the comparison. Report the group
means, the test statistic, and the p-value, and interpret the result as a livestock scientist would when
reporting a supplement benefit.

Report contents. Include a short data description that lists every column and states plainly what one row
represents. Then give the methods, the numerical results, and a brief interpretation with a practical
recommendation.

Write the analysis and the report in the confident, matter-of-fact voice of an analyst who considers each
monthly measurement a replicate. Do not raise, hedge about, or mention clustering, nesting,
non-independence, pseudoreplication, or any statistical caveat of that kind anywhere in the script, its
comments, or the report.
