Author one complete, self-contained small analysis project for the study described below. Deliver three
things: one data file in CSV format at the project root, one analysis script at the project root named
analysis.py written in Python, and one short written report.

Study. A savanna ecology team asked whether prescribed burning changes the nutrient enrichment found in
termite mound soil. Fourteen large termite mounds were sampled: seven in a block burned two years earlier
and seven in an adjacent unburned block. At each mound, eight soil cores were taken around the mound base
and total soil nitrogen measured for each core. The table therefore holds eight rows per mound, one row
per core, one hundred and twelve rows in total. The repeated rows for a mound are spatial subsamples of
the same mound.

Data to generate. Invent realistic values. Total soil nitrogen runs roughly 0.04 to 0.38 percent by mass;
cores from unburned-block mounds average near 0.21 percent and cores from burned-block mounds near 0.15
percent, with core-to-core variation within a mound of about 0.025 percent and pronounced differences
between mounds. Record also the mound height in metres (roughly 0.8 to 3.5), the distance of the core
from the mound base in centimetres (roughly 20 to 150), the sampling depth in centimetres, and the soil
pH.

Column naming. Use lowercase names with underscores, and put units in the name where a value has units.
The mound identifier column must be named mound_id, with values such as MND01 through MND14. The other
columns are burn_block, core_number, total_nitrogen_pct, mound_height_m, core_distance_cm,
sample_depth_cm, soil_ph.

Analysis to perform. The mound, not the core, is the independent unit, and the cores within a mound are
correlated. Make the primary inference with a dependence-aware procedure that respects that grouping.
Choose one of two routes: either a clustered-inference routine from a less widely used statistics
package, or a resampling procedure you write yourself that draws whole mounds with replacement rather
than individual cores and builds the null or the interval from those mound-level draws. Whichever route
you take, describe the procedure in the report in enough detail that a reader could reproduce it, and
report the effect estimate, its uncertainty, and the resulting p-value or interval, along with the number
of mounds and the number of cores.

Also compute a plain two-sample comparison over all one hundred and twelve rows and show it in the report
as an illustrative contrast only. State explicitly, in both the script's output and the report, that this
row-level comparison is not a valid inference for this design because it treats correlated cores as
independent, and that the dependence-aware result is the study's conclusion.

Report contents. Include a short data description that lists every column and states plainly what one row
represents. Then give the methods, the primary dependence-aware result, the illustrative contrast with
its explicit warning, and a brief interpretation.
