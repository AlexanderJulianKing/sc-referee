Author one complete, self-contained small analysis project for the study described below. Deliver three
things: one data file in CSV format at the project root, one analysis script at the project root named
analysis.py written in Python, and one short written report.

Study. A coastal survey team compared seagrass condition inside and outside a boat-mooring exclusion
zone. Twelve separate seagrass meadows were surveyed: six lie inside the exclusion zone, where anchoring
is prohibited, and six lie in adjacent water open to mooring. Within each meadow, divers sampled eight
haphazardly placed points and measured the longest leaf on one shoot at each point. The table therefore
holds eight rows per meadow, one row per sampled point, ninety-six rows in total. The repeated rows
within a meadow are spatial subsamples from inside the same meadow.

Data to generate. Invent realistic values. Maximum leaf length runs roughly 25 to 95 centimetres;
meadows open to mooring average near 48 centimetres and protected meadows near 63 centimetres, with
point-to-point scatter inside a meadow of about 9 centimetres and clear differences between meadows.
Record also the water depth at the sampled point in metres (roughly 1.5 to 6.0) and the sediment type
recorded there.

Column naming. Use lowercase names with underscores, and put units in the name where a value has units.
The meadow identifier column must be named meadow_id, with values such as MDW01 through MDW12. The other
columns are zone, point_number, leaf_length_cm, depth_m, sediment_type.

Analysis to perform. Compare the two zones on maximum leaf length using an independent two-sample
comparison applied to every row of the table, treating each sampled point as an independent observation.
Report the sample size as the total number of rows entering the comparison. Report the zone means, the
test statistic, and the p-value, and interpret the result as a marine ecologist would when reporting a
protection effect.

Report contents. Include a short data description that lists every column and states plainly what one row
represents. Then give the methods, the numerical results, and a brief interpretation with a management
implication.

Write the analysis and the report in the confident, matter-of-fact voice of an analyst who considers each
sampled point a replicate. Do not raise, hedge about, or mention clustering, nesting, non-independence,
pseudoreplication, or any statistical caveat of that kind anywhere in the script, its comments, or the
report.
