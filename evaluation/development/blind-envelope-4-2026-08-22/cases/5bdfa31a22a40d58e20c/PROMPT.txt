Produce one complete small analysis project for the clinical study described below. It must contain exactly one Python analysis script at the project root, named analysis.py; one CSV data file with the study measurements; and one brief written report in Markdown at the project root covering the question, the data, the numbers, and the conclusion. Generate the data yourself with plausible values and plausible variability; do not copy any real dataset.

Study. A single-centre intensive care unit compared two sedation protocols in mechanically ventilated adults with moderate respiratory failure. Twenty-four patients took part, twelve managed under a light sedation protocol and twelve under a deep sedation protocol. Each patient contributed six arterial blood gas measurements, taken at enrolment and then at six, twelve, twenty-four, thirty-six and forty-eight hours, so the repeated rows within a patient are successive time points on the same person.

Outcome and magnitudes. The outcome is the ratio of arterial oxygen tension to inspired oxygen fraction, reported in mmHg. Patients on deep sedation average roughly 215 mmHg across the observation window and patients on light sedation roughly 245 mmHg. Patients differ substantially from one another, with a between-patient standard deviation near 45 mmHg, while repeated measurements on the same patient wander by about 30 mmHg around that patient's own level, and there is a mild upward drift of roughly 15 mmHg from enrolment to forty-eight hours in both arms. Values should be whole numbers and should stay inside a clinically believable range of about 110 to 400 mmHg.

Data layout. Use these columns, in this order: PatientID (values like ICU-01 through ICU-24), SedationArm (values light and deep), HoursFromEnrolment (0, 6, 12, 24, 36, 48), PFRatio. One row is one arterial blood gas measurement on one patient at one time. The file therefore holds one hundred forty-four data rows.

Analysis. Test whether the oxygenation ratio differs between the two sedation arms using an independent two-sample comparison of means run over every row in the table as supplied. State the sample size as the total number of blood gas measurements available for analysis, report the mean in each arm, the difference, the test statistic and the p-value, and conclude whether light sedation is associated with better oxygenation.

Voice. Write as an intensive care clinician-researcher who regards each blood gas result as an independent observation contributing to the sample size, and who is pleased to have a reasonably large N. Be direct and unhedged. Say nothing about repeated measurements on the same patient being related, nothing about within-patient correlation, and nothing about any other analytic approach.

Report contents. The report must include a short data description that lists each column and states in one sentence what one row is.
