Assemble one complete small analysis project for the sea turtle study below. Deliver exactly one Python analysis script at the project root, named analysis.py, one CSV data file, and one short Markdown report at the project root that states the question, describes the data, presents the results, and draws the conclusion. Study. A nesting beach monitoring programme compared two incubation substrates used in a protected hatchery: native beach sand and a coarser imported sand used where the natural profile has eroded. Twenty-four green turtle clutches were relocated to the hatchery, twelve into each substrate. After emergence, ten hatchlings from each clutch were measured before release, so the repeated rows within a clutch are individual hatchlings from the same mother's single clutch.

Outcome and magnitudes. The outcome is straight carapace length in millimetres. Hatchlings from native sand average about 43.6 mm and hatchlings from imported sand about 41.2 mm. Clutches differ strongly from one another because mothers differ in size and egg quality, with a between-clutch standard deviation near 1.8 mm, while hatchlings within one clutch vary by about 1.2 mm around their own clutch mean. Record lengths to one decimal place and keep them inside a believable range of about 36 to 49 mm.

Data layout. Use these columns, in this order: clutch_ref (values like CL-01 through CL-24), substrate (values native and imported), hatchling_number (1 through 10 within a clutch), carapace_length_mm. One row is one measured hatchling. The CSV holds two hundred forty data rows.

Analysis. Hatchlings from one clutch share a mother, an egg chamber and a thermal history, so they cannot be treated as independent draws. The primary inference must use a procedure that respects clutch-level dependence, and you should implement it in one of two ways: either fit a dependence-aware model using a statistics package that is not the default general-purpose scientific one bundled with most installations, or write your own resampling procedure that repeatedly draws whole clutches with replacement, recomputes the substrate difference within each resample, and forms a confidence interval and a p-value from that distribution of clutch-level resamples. Whichever route you take, describe in the report exactly what the procedure resamples or models, and report the number of clutches as well as the number of hatchlings.

Illustrative contrast. Also compute a plain independent two-sample comparison of means over the raw hatchling rows and show it beside the primary result, purely to illustrate how much narrower and more confident that naive comparison looks. The report must state in plain terms that this hatchling-level comparison treats siblings as independent replicates and is therefore not a valid basis for inference here, and that the study's conclusion rests on the clutch-aware procedure alone.

Reporting. Give the mean carapace length under each substrate, the primary effect estimate with its interval and p-value, the contrasting naive p-value, and the conclusion. Include a short data description listing every column and stating in one sentence what a single row represents.
