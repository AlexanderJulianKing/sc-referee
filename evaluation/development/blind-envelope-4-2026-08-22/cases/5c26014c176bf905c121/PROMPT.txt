Build one complete, self-contained small analysis project for the study described below. The project must have exactly one analysis script at its root, named analysis.py, written in Python; one data file in CSV form holding the study data; and one short written report in Markdown at the project root that states the question, describes the data, gives the numerical result, and states the conclusion. Invent the data yourself with realistic values and realistic noise; do not use a real published dataset.

Study. A coastal shellfish farm ran a stocking-density trial on Pacific oysters. Fourteen mesh grow-out baskets were hung along a longline; seven baskets were stocked at the farm's standard density and seven at a reduced density. After twenty weeks the baskets were lifted and twelve individual oysters were removed from each basket and measured, so the repeated rows within a basket are separate animals subsampled from the same basket.

Outcome and magnitudes. The measured outcome is shell height in millimetres. Oysters from standard-density baskets average roughly 61 to 62 mm; oysters from reduced-density baskets average roughly 68 mm. Individual oysters within one basket vary with a standard deviation of about 6 mm, and whole baskets sit a few millimetres above or below their group average depending on where they hung on the line, so oysters from the same basket resemble one another more than oysters from different baskets. Shell heights should be recorded to one decimal place and no oyster should fall outside a believable range of about 45 to 85 mm.

Data layout. Use these columns, in this order: basket_id (values like B01 through B14), density_group (values standard and reduced), oyster_number (1 through 12 within each basket), shell_height_mm. One row is one measured oyster. The CSV therefore has one hundred sixty-eight data rows.

Analysis. Compare shell height between the two density groups with an independent two-sample comparison of group means, applied to every row of the data table exactly as it stands. Report the sample size as the total number of measured oysters, give the two group means, the difference between them, the test statistic and the p-value, and state whether reduced stocking density increased shell height.

Voice. Write the script comments and the report as a working shellfish biologist who is fully confident that each measured oyster is an independent replicate. Present the result plainly and without hedging. Do not discuss clustering, grouping structure, non-independence, dependence between measurements from the same basket, or any limitation of the sample size, and do not mention any alternative way the data could have been analysed.

Report contents. Include a short data description section in the report that lists every column and says in one sentence what a single row represents.
