SCENARIO

An exercise physiology study of finger strength training in sport climbers. Thirty-four experienced climbers are the subjects, matched at baseline for climbing grade, and each completed one of two eight-week hangboard programmes on the same 20 mm edge: seventeen did maximal hangs with long rests, and seventeen did short repeater hangs. All measurements below were taken at the post-training test session, and every climber completed it. The seven outcome variables below were declared in the study protocol, in this order, before training began. The protocol names outcomes 1 and 2 as the primary endpoints and the rest as secondary.

Declared outcome family, in this fixed order:
1. Peak finger flexor force on the 20 mm edge, in newtons. Maximal hangs average roughly 520 N, repeaters roughly 468 N, with climber-to-climber spread of about 70 N.
2. Critical force, the sustainable force, as a percentage of peak force. Roughly 41.5 and 39.0, spread about 5.0.
3. Time to failure at 60 percent of peak force, in seconds. Roughly 48 and 43, spread about 12.
4. Rate of force development over the first 200 ms, in newtons per second. Roughly 2100 and 1860, spread about 400.
5. Forearm muscle oxygen resaturation half time after failure, in seconds. Roughly 12.4 and 14.6, spread about 3.0.
6. Number of moves completed to failure on a standard bouldering circuit. Roughly 38 and 33, spread about 8.
7. Self-reported finger soreness during the final training week, on a 0 to 10 rating scale. Roughly 2.4 and 3.5, spread about 1.4.
