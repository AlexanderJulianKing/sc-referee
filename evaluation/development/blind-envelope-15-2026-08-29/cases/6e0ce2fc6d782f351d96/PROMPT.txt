SCENARIO

A smoking cessation service evaluation comparing two nicotine substitution options. Seventy-two adult daily smokers who asked for help quitting are the subjects. Thirty-six were allocated to a refillable nicotine vaping device with standardised liquid strength, and thirty-six to transdermal nicotine patches at a standard starting dose. Both groups received the same brief behavioural support. All measurements below were taken at the week six visit, and every participant attended. The three outcome variables below were declared in the evaluation protocol, in this order, before recruitment started.

Declared outcome family, in this fixed order:
1. Exhaled carbon monoxide at week six, in parts per million. The vaping group averages roughly 7.5 ppm and the patch group roughly 10.6 ppm, with person-to-person spread of about 4.5 ppm.
2. Cigarettes smoked per day at week six, self-reported over the previous seven days. Roughly 3.4 in the vaping group and 5.9 in the patch group, spread about 3.0.
3. Strongest urge to smoke in the past week, on a 0 to 100 visual analogue scale. Roughly 33 and 40, spread about 16.
