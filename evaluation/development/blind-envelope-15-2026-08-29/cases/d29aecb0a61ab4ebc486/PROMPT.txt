SCENARIO

A cereal processing study on pasta drying temperature. Fifty production lots of the same durum semolina spaghetti are the experimental subjects, each lot extruded and dried separately on the same line and sampled once after drying. Twenty-five lots were dried on a low temperature cycle peaking at 55 degrees Celsius and twenty-five on a very high temperature cycle peaking at 90 degrees Celsius; formulation, extrusion and packaging were identical. The five outcome variables below were declared in the study plan, in this order, before the first lot was run.

Declared outcome family, in this fixed order:
1. Cooking loss, as a percentage of dry weight lost to the cooking water. Low temperature lots average roughly 6.8 and high temperature lots roughly 5.4, with lot-to-lot spread of about 1.0.
2. Optimal cooking time, in minutes. Roughly 10.5 and 9.6, spread about 0.8.
3. Firmness of the cooked strand, in newtons. Roughly 1.85 and 2.15, spread about 0.30.
4. Colour yellowness on the b star axis. Roughly 28.5 and 25.6, spread about 2.0.
5. Furosine content as a marker of heat damage, in milligrams per 100 g of protein. Roughly 120 and 320, spread about 60.
