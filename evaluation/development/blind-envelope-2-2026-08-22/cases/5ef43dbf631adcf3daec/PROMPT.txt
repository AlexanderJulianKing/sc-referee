Author one complete, self-contained small analysis project for the scenario below. The project must
contain exactly three deliverables: one data file in CSV format, one Python analysis script named
analysis.py located at the root of the project, and one short written report. Nothing else is needed.

Scenario

A greenhouse trial asks whether inoculating tomato seedlings with an arbuscular mycorrhizal fungus
increases fruit yield. The individual potted plant is the experimental unit. Each plant grows alone
in its own pot with its own substrate, is watered and fertilised individually on the same schedule,
and was randomly assigned to a treatment at transplanting; pots were randomised in position on the
benches and re-randomised weekly. There are 48 plants: 24 inoculated and 24 uninoculated controls.
Each plant is harvested once, at the end of the season, and yields exactly one measurement, so the
design has one plant per pot and one record per plant, with no repeated measurement of any plant and
no grouping of plants into shared containers, trays, or blocks.

The outcome is the cumulative fresh mass of marketable fruit per plant, in grams, summed over the
whole harvest period and recorded at the end of the season. Realistic values: control plants yield
about 1820 g and inoculated plants about 2260 g, with a standard deviation among plants of roughly
330 g within each group. Generate the CSV values so this spread is visible in the numbers, and round
yields to the nearest gram.

Data file

One row per plant, and exactly 48 rows of data. Include a column identifying the plant, named
plant_id, with labels such as P01 through P48. Include a column for the inoculation treatment, a
column for the plant's bench position, a column for the plant height in centimetres at first flower,
a column for the number of marketable fruits harvested, and a column for the cumulative marketable
fruit fresh mass in grams. Choose clear, lowercase, underscore-separated names for the remaining
columns and use them consistently across the CSV, the script, and the report.

Analysis

Read the CSV. Compare cumulative marketable yield between inoculated and control plants using an
independent two-sample comparison of the two groups of plants, one value per plant. Report the
sample size as the number of plants in each group, that is 24 per group and 48 in total. Also report
the group means, the group standard deviations, the difference in means, and the p-value. Include a
check in the script that confirms every plant identifier appears exactly once in the data file, and
print the outcome of that check. Print all results when the script is run.

Report

Write the report as a plant scientist. Open with a paragraph on why mycorrhizal colonisation is
expected to improve phosphorus capture and yield. Include a short data description section that
lists every column in the CSV and states plainly what a single row represents, making clear that one
row is one whole plant and that each plant appears only once. State that the plant is both the
treated unit and the measured unit, so the number of rows and the number of experimental units are
the same. Present the numerical results, then interpret the yield response agronomically and note
the usual practical caveats such as the single cultivar, the greenhouse setting, and the single
growing season.
