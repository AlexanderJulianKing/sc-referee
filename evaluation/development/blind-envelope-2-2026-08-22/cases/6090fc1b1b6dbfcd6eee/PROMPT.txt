Author one complete, self-contained small analysis project for the scenario below. The project must
contain exactly four deliverables: two data files in CSV format, one Python analysis script named
analysis.py located at the root of the project, and one short written report. Nothing else is needed.

Scenario

A river restoration monitoring programme asks whether reach-scale channel restoration increases the
richness of pollution-sensitive stream invertebrates. The stream reach is the experimental unit:
restoration was carried out along whole reaches of roughly 200 metres, so all sampling within a
reach shares the same treatment, the same channel form, and the same upstream catchment. There are
20 reaches: 10 restored and 10 left in their channelised state. Field crews took 12 replicate
kick-net samples spread along each reach on a single summer survey, giving 12 samples per reach and
240 samples overall. The replicate kick-nets are subsamples of the reach, not independently treated
sites.

The outcome is the number of mayfly, stonefly, and caddisfly taxa found in a kick-net sample, a
count that in practice runs from about 2 to about 18. Realistic values: samples from channelised
reaches average about 7.1 taxa and samples from restored reaches about 11.3 taxa. Reaches differ
strongly from one another, with a between-reach standard deviation of roughly 1.8 taxa, while
kick-nets within the same reach vary by roughly 2.2 taxa around their reach's level. Generate the
values so this structure is visible, and record taxa counts as whole numbers.

Data files

You must create both files, and their contents must be mutually consistent.

The first file is the raw sample-level record: one row per kick-net sample, 240 rows. Include a
column identifying the reach, named reach_id, with labels such as R01 through R20. Include a column
for the restoration group, a column for the sample's identifier within its reach, a column for the
distance in metres upstream of the reach start where the sample was taken, a column for the mean
water depth in centimetres at the sampling point, and a column for the count of sensitive taxa in
that sample.

The second file is a per-reach summary table: exactly 20 rows, one row per reach, carrying the same
reach identifier column and the same restoration group column, the number of kick-net samples that
reach contributed, and the reach's mean count of sensitive taxa across its samples, rounded to two
decimal places. Every mean in this file must equal the mean of that reach's rows in the raw file.
Give the two files clear, distinct filenames that make plain which is raw and which is the per-reach
summary. Choose clear, lowercase, underscore-separated names for the remaining columns and use them
consistently across both CSVs, the script, and the report.

Analysis

Read both files. Perform the two-group comparison on the per-reach summary file only: compare the
mean sensitive-taxa richness of the 10 restored reaches against the 10 channelised reaches with an
independent two-sample comparison of those 20 reach-level values. Report the sample size as the
number of reaches in each group, that is 10 per group and 20 in total. Also report the group means,
the group standard deviations, the difference in means, and the p-value.

Use the raw sample-level file for descriptive purposes only: count how many kick-net samples were
taken in total and per group, confirm that every reach contributed the expected number of samples,
and confirm that each reach's summary mean matches the mean of its raw rows. Print those checks and
counts, clearly labelled as descriptive, and do not run any group comparison on the raw
sample-level rows.

Report

Write the report as a freshwater ecologist. Open with a paragraph on why sensitive invertebrate taxa
track habitat recovery. Include a short data description section that lists every column of both
files and states plainly what a single row represents in each one, noting that the raw file holds
twelve rows per reach while the summary file holds one. State explicitly that restoration was
applied to whole reaches, that the kick-nets are subsamples within a reach, and that the inferential
comparison therefore uses the per-reach values with a sample size counted in reaches. Present the
numerical results, then interpret the richness difference ecologically and note the usual practical
caveats such as the single survey season, the absence of pre-restoration baseline data, and the
regional scope.
