Author one complete, self-contained small analysis project for the scenario below. The project must
contain exactly three deliverables: one data file in CSV format, one Python analysis script named
analysis.py located at the root of the project, and one short written report. Nothing else is needed.

Scenario

A behavioural ecology study asks whether chronic sublethal exposure to a common fungicide reduces
the size of the leaf fragments carried by leaf-cutter ant foragers. The whole ant colony is the
experimental unit. Each queenright colony was maintained in its own foraging arena and the fungicide
was delivered through that colony's forage supply, so every worker in a colony shares the same
exposure, the same queen, and the same fungus garden. There are 16 colonies: 8 exposed and 8
unexposed controls. From each colony, 10 returning foragers were intercepted on the trail and the
leaf fragment each was carrying was weighed, giving 10 weighed foragers per colony and 160 weighed
foragers overall.

The outcome is the fresh mass of the carried leaf fragment in milligrams. Realistic values: foragers
from control colonies carry fragments of about 22.5 mg and foragers from exposed colonies about
18.4 mg. Colonies differ markedly from one another, with a between-colony standard deviation of
roughly 2.2 mg, while foragers within the same colony vary by roughly 4.0 mg around their colony's
level. Generate the CSV values so this structure is visible in the numbers, and round masses to one
decimal place. Fragment masses must stay positive.

Data file

One row per intercepted forager, so a colony appears on ten rows. Include a column identifying the
colony, named colony_id, with labels such as C01 through C16. Include a column for the exposure
group, a column for the forager's identifier within its colony, a column for the head width of the
worker in millimetres, a column for the hour of interception, and a column for the leaf fragment
fresh mass in milligrams. Choose clear, lowercase, underscore-separated names for the remaining
columns and use them consistently across the CSV, the script, and the report.

Analysis

Read the CSV. The primary inference must respect the fact that foragers are nested within colonies.
Fit a model of fragment mass on exposure group that includes a colony-level random effect, so that
variation between colonies is estimated separately from variation between foragers within a colony,
and report the estimated exposure effect, its standard error, its confidence interval, and its
p-value from that model, along with the estimated between-colony and within-colony variance
components. Treat this model as the inferential result of the project.

Then, and only as a secondary sensitivity check, also run a plain independent two-sample comparison
of the 160 individual forager records that ignores colony membership, and print its result. The
script's printed output must label the mixed model as the primary analysis and label the row-level
comparison as a secondary sensitivity analysis that ignores colony structure and is not the basis
for any conclusion. Print both sets of numbers when the script is run.

Report

Write the report as a behavioural ecologist. Open with a paragraph on why forager load size is a
sensitive indicator of colony condition. Include a short data description section that lists every
column in the CSV and states plainly what a single row represents, noting that each colony
contributes ten rows. State explicitly that the colony is the unit that received the treatment and
that the primary analysis therefore models colony as a random effect. Report the primary model
results first and base every conclusion on them. Present the row-level comparison afterwards, in a
clearly subordinate subsection, described as a sensitivity check that ignores the nesting and
overstates the number of independent observations, and say plainly that it is not the inferential
result. Close with biological interpretation and the usual practical caveats such as the single
fungicide, the laboratory arenas, and the single sampling season.
