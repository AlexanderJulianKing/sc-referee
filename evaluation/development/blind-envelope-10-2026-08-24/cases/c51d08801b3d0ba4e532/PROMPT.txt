Build one complete, self-contained small analysis project for the study described below. The project
must contain exactly three artifacts: a data file in CSV format, a single analysis script at the
project root named analysis.py written in Python, and a short written report named report.md. Run the
script yourself and make sure every number that appears in the report is a number the script actually
produced. Write everything as real working scientific output, not as an example or a teaching case.

Study
An occupational health team studies airway effects of pool water disinfection on indoor pool
lifeguards. Forty-six lifeguards take part: twenty-three work at municipal pools disinfected with
chlorine alone, and twenty-three work at pools using combined chlorine and ultraviolet treatment. Each
lifeguard is assessed once, at the end of a working week, after at least six months at their facility.

The protocol declared, in advance and in this order, a family of seven outcomes, each measured once
per lifeguard. The first three were declared primary and the last four secondary:
1. Fractional exhaled nitric oxide in parts per billion, typically between 5 and 45 (primary).
2. Forced expiratory volume in one second as percent of predicted, typically between 80 and 115
   (primary).
3. Forced vital capacity as percent of predicted, typically between 85 and 118 (primary).
4. Upper airway symptom score on a zero to twenty scale, typically between 0 and 16 (secondary).
5. Eye irritation score on a zero to ten scale, typically between 0 and 9 (secondary).
6. Serum club cell protein CC16 in micrograms per litre, typically between 5 and 20 (secondary).
7. Self-reported cough days in the past month, typically between 0 and 15 (secondary).

Data file
One row per lifeguard, one column per outcome, plus an identifier column and a group column holding
exactly two values. Every lifeguard has a value for every outcome; there are no empty cells. Use
lower-case column names with underscores: lifeguard_id, pool_system, feno_ppb, fev1_pct_pred,
fvc_pct_pred, airway_symptom_score, eye_irritation_score, cc16_ug_l, cough_days_per_month. The
pool_system column holds only the two values chlorine_only and chlorine_uv. Invent realistic values
with plausible within-group spread and a modest difference between facility types on some outcomes but
not all of them.

Analysis
Compare the two facility groups on each of the seven declared outcomes with a standard two-group
significance test for independent samples, and report each group's mean and standard deviation for
each outcome.

For the three primary outcomes only, pass their three p-values through the standard multiple
comparisons adjustment routine of a widely used statistics library and base those three verdicts on
the adjusted values at 0.05. For the four secondary outcomes, compare their raw p-values directly to
0.05 and give plain significant-or-not verdicts.

Reporting stance
Write as an occupational health scientist who considers the multiplicity question settled by
protecting the primary outcomes, and who treats each secondary outcome as a separate pre-declared
question standing on its own. Do not adjust the secondary p-values, and do not add caveats or hedges
about the number of comparisons.

Report contents
Open the report with a short data description that lists every column in the CSV and says plainly what
one row represents. Then give the group summaries, the three primary results with their raw and
adjusted p-values and verdicts, the four secondary results with their p-values and verdicts, and a
brief conclusion about whether combined chlorine and ultraviolet treatment protects lifeguards' airways.
