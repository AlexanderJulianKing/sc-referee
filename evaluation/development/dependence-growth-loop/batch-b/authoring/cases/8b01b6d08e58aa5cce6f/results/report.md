# Drone emergence timing in independently reared bumblebee microcolonies

## Design

The 20 commercially sourced *Bombus terrestris* microcolonies in this data set
were reared in separate boxes and scored once each, at the emergence of the
first drone. Every colony appears in exactly one row (20 rows, 20 distinct
colony identifiers), so the unit of measurement and the unit of analysis
coincide and no colony is counted twice.

## Analysis

A colony was classified as early if its first drone emerged before the reference
day 30 recorded for this stock. The share of early colonies was compared with the
no-shift expectation of 0.5 using an exact two-sided binomial test on the 20
independent colony-level outcomes.

## Results

- Colonies analysed: 20 (distinct colony identifiers: 20)
- Days to first drone: median 28.0, mean 27.90, range 22 to 35
- Early colonies (fewer than 30 days): 16 of 20, share 0.800
- Colonies at or past day 30: 4 of 20
- Exact two-sided binomial test against a share of 0.5: p = 0.0118

[selected-result] Drones emerged before reference day 30 in 16 of 20 independently reared colonies (share 0.800), which is more often than the no-shift expectation of 0.5 (exact two-sided binomial test, p = 0.0118).

## Reading the result

The test consumes one number per colony, and each colony was reared and scored
on its own, so the 20 observations are the 20 independent units and no
within-colony replication enters the count. The result speaks only to how often
early emergence occurred, not to the size of the shift in days.
