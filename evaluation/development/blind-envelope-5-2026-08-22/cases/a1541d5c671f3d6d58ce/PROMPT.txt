Build one complete, self-contained analysis project for the study described below. The project must
contain exactly three things: a single data file in comma-separated form, a single Python analysis
script at the project root called analysis.py, and a short written report in Markdown at the project
root. Nothing else. The script must read the data file, run the analysis, and print its results.

Study. A postharvest laboratory compared two storage atmospheres for seed potatoes. Fourteen sealed
storage bins were each filled from the same graded lot: seven bins were held under conventional
cold air and seven under a low-oxygen controlled atmosphere. Every bin was opened and sampled at six
storage times, at four-week intervals from week 4 to week 24, so each bin contributes six time
points and the file holds 84 records in total. The outcome is tuber firmness measured with a
penetrometer, in newtons. Realistic firmness starts near 62 to 70 newtons and declines over the
storage season, falling to roughly 40 to 48 newtons by week 24 under conventional air and roughly 50
to 58 newtons under the low-oxygen atmosphere, with each bin holding its own consistent level above
or below its group's trend. Also record the weight loss since loading as a percentage, rising from
under 1 percent to roughly 4 to 9 percent by the end.

Data file. One line per bin visit. Columns, in this order and with these exact headers: bin_code,
atmosphere, storage_week, weight_loss_pct, firmness_newton. Bin codes should look like real store
labels. Round firmness to one decimal.

Analysis. Compare the two storage atmospheres on tuber firmness with an independent two-sample test
of the difference in means, passing every record in the table into the comparison as a separate
observation. Report the sample size as the number of firmness records analysed, give each group's
mean and spread, and state the p-value and the conclusion about the atmosphere.

Report. Keep it short. Begin with a data description that lists every column and says plainly what
one row of the file represents. Then give the methods, the numbers, and a brief recommendation for
next season's storage, written in the ordinary confident voice of a postharvest scientist. Do not
question, caveat, or discuss the sample size you used.
