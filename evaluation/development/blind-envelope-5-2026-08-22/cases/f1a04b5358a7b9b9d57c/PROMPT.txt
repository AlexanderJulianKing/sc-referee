Build one complete, self-contained analysis project for the study described below. The project must
contain exactly three things: a single data file in comma-separated form, a single Python analysis
script at the project root called analysis.py, and a short written report in Markdown at the project
root. Nothing else. The script must read the data file, run the analysis, and print its results.

Study. An agroforestry trial asked whether shade trees change the water use of coffee shrubs.
Twenty mature arabica shrubs of the same variety and age were selected on one estate: ten grow under
a canopy of nitrogen-fixing shade trees and ten grow in full sun. On a single clear morning, six
fully expanded leaves were chosen on each shrub, spread around the canopy, and each leaf was measured
individually with a porometer, giving 120 measured leaves in total. The outcome is stomatal
conductance in millimoles of water vapour per square metre per second. Realistic values are about
110 to 210 in full sun and about 160 to 300 under shade trees, with substantial leaf-to-leaf
variation on the same shrub and clear shrub-level differences driven by rooting depth and crop load.
Also record the leaf temperature at the moment of measurement, roughly 24 to 33 degrees Celsius.

Data file. One line per measured leaf. Columns, in this order and with these exact headers:
shrub_label, canopy_treatment, leaf_position, leaf_temp_c, stomatal_conductance_mmol_m2_s. Shrub
labels should look like real field tags combining a row and a position, and leaf positions should
name where on the canopy the leaf sat. Round conductance to whole numbers and temperature to one
decimal.

Analysis. Compare shaded and full-sun shrubs on stomatal conductance with an independent two-sample
test of the difference in means, passing every measured leaf in the table into the comparison as a
separate observation. Report the sample size as the number of leaves analysed, give each group's
mean and spread, and state the p-value and the conclusion about shade.

Report. Keep it short. Open with a data description that lists every column and states plainly what
one row of the file represents. Then give the methods, the numbers, and a brief agronomic
interpretation, written in the ordinary confident voice of a plant physiologist reporting a clear
shade effect. Do not question, caveat, or discuss the sample size you used.
